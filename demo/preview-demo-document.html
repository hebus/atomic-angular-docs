Q3 2026 — Product Roadmap Review

Prepared by the Product team · Internal · September 2026

1. Executive summary

The third quarter closed with four of six planned milestones delivered. Search relevance and the new document workspaces shipped on schedule; the mobile redesign and the audit export slipped by one sprint. This document reviews what was delivered, what moved, and what we commit to for the fourth quarter.

“Ship fewer things, but ship them finished.” — guiding principle adopted at the start of the quarter.

3. Delivered this quarter

  1. Search relevance Done — new ranking profile, validated on 1 200 real queries.
  2. Document workspaces Done — create, share and search a set of documents.
  3. Single sign-on Done — OIDC with automatic account linking.
  4. Accessibility pass Done — keyboard navigation and contrast on every filter.

4. Slipped to next sprint

  • Mobile redesign In progress — navigation done, filters panel remaining.
  • Audit export At risk — depends on the new retention policy.

The slippage is mostly explained by the late arrival of the retention requirements. The team has agreed to freeze the scope of both items until the policy is signed off, to avoid rework.

5. Risks

Risk Likelihood Mitigation
Retention policy delayed again Medium Ship the export behind a feature flag with a configurable retention.
Index growth outpaces capacity Low Provision two additional shards before the October peak.

6. Commitments for Q4

The following items are committed. Anything not listed here is, by definition, a stretch goal.

  1. Complete the mobile redesign, including the filters panel.
  2. Release the audit export with configurable retention.
  3. Open the workspaces to external readers.
  4. Reduce the median search latency under 250 ms.

7. Decisions needed

Two decisions are required from the steering committee before the end of October: the retention period applied by default, and whether external readers count towards the licence quota.

Thank you to everyone who contributed to this quarter — particularly the support team, whose feedback shaped most of the accessibility work.
